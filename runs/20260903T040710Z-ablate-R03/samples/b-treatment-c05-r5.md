I didn't find the cause, but the run history narrows it considerably and points at where to look next.

The short runs are not random. Of the last eight runs, three wrote roughly half the rows (23902, 22104, 23551) and each of those finished in about seven minutes, while the full runs took fifteen or sixteen. So the job isn't dropping rows it fetched; it's fetching about half as many events and processing them correctly. Every run is marked `ok`, which fits: nothing is erroring, the window is just smaller. The short runs fall on 2025-08-23, 08-26 and 08-29, a clean three-day spacing that suggests something periodic rather than a race, though three points is thin evidence and I'd want the full thirty rows before trusting the pattern.

The most likely mechanism is the window calculation in `jobs/scheduler.py:61`: `window_start = last_success_at or (now - timedelta(hours=24))`. The window starts at the previous run's success timestamp, not at a fixed 24-hour boundary, so anything that moves `last_success_at` forward shortens the next window. A retry or a manual re-run partway through the day would record a success and leave the following night covering only the hours since then. The recent commit `9d02e1e` ("retry transient fetch errors") is a plausible source of exactly that, if a retried attempt writes its own `job_runs` row on success.

What would confirm or kill this: pull `started_at`, `finished_at` and `rows_written` for all thirty runs and check whether a short run is always preceded by an extra `job_runs` entry earlier that same day, and separately compute `window_start`/`window_end` per run. Those two columns aren't recorded in `job_runs` today, and logging them would turn this from inference into a direct reading. I also haven't checked whether `fetch_events` bounds are inclusive or exclusive at the edges, or whether `last_success_at` and `now` come from the same clock and timezone; a UTC/local mismatch would produce a similar shortfall, though it would do so on every run rather than intermittently, which is why the window-reset explanation fits the data better.
