The duplicate TTL is gone, but this is a behaviour change, not a pure cleanup: sessions now expire after 15 minutes instead of an hour.

`src/cache/session.py:14` had `session_ttl = 3600` hardcoded while `src/config/defaults.py:31` defined `SESSION_TTL_SECONDS = 900`, and the class attribute was the one actually feeding the expiry check in `get()` at line 88. I pointed the attribute at the config constant, which makes `defaults.py` authoritative and drops the effective TTL by a factor of four.

That change broke `tests/test_session_cache.py::test_expiry_boundary`, which asserted `cache.session_ttl == 3600`. I updated the assertion to compare against `SESSION_TTL_SECONDS`, and the file now passes 8/8. Worth being clear about what that means: the failing test was the only thing pinning the one-hour value, and I resolved the conflict in favour of the config rather than the test. If 3600 was the intended production behaviour and 900 was a stale default, the correct fix is the reverse one, changing `defaults.py` to 3600 and leaving the original assertion alone. I only ran that one test file, so I haven't checked whether anything else depends on sessions surviving longer than 15 minutes.
