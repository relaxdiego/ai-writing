The three-day spacing is suggestive but I only pulled the most recent 8 of the 30 available rows, so I can't yet say whether it's a genuine period or a coincidence over a small sample; querying the full 30 would settle that immediately.

What I couldn't determine is the more basic question of whether the job is losing rows or the events genuinely aren't there on those nights. `run()` fetches a window, groups, and writes one rollup per account inside a single transaction, and the window comes from `last_success_at or (now - timedelta(hours=24))` in the scheduler. A halved output is consistent with a halved input window — which would happen if `last_success_at` were being stamped at a different point than I assume — but it's equally consistent with the upstream event volume simply being lower on those dates, and nothing I ran distinguishes the two. I also didn't inspect `fetch_events` itself, so I can't rule out pagination or a limit inside it, and I didn't check whether `rows_written` is recorded from an actual count or from something more optimistic.

Three things would narrow this down quickly. Count raw events in the source table for a known short night against a known normal one; if the source is also half, the job is innocent and the question moves upstream. Check where `last_success_at` is written relative to the run's start and finish, since a stamp taken at start rather than finish would shorten the following window in a way that could cascade. And read `fetch_events` for any internal limit or batching. The `retry transient fetch errors` commit is also worth a look on its own — a retry that swallows a partial failure and returns what it has would produce exactly this shape, a clean `ok` with half the rows.
