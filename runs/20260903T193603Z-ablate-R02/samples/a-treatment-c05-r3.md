I didn't find the cause. Here's what the evidence does and doesn't support, and where I'd point the next hour.

The shortfall is real and it's close to exactly half: 23902 against 48213 the following night, 22104 against 48009, 23551 against 47760. The important detail is that the short runs are also short in wall-clock time, about 6m40s to 7m12s against 14m51s to 16m33s for the full ones, and the ratio of durations tracks the ratio of rows. That points away from the write path. If `write_rollup` were failing silently or the transaction in `jobs/nightly_rollup.py:23` were rolling back part of its work, the job would still have spent the time fetching and grouping the full set. It didn't. Fewer rows came back from `fetch_events`, so the problem is upstream of the loop: either the window is wrong or the source table is short at the moment we read it.

The window is the obvious suspect. `jobs/scheduler.py:61` computes `window_start` from `last_success_at`, falling back to 24 hours. I never found where `last_success_at` is written, only where it is read, so I can't tell you whether it's set from `started_at`, from `finished_at`, or on some path that also fires for partial or retried runs. The retry commit, `9d02e1e`, is the kind of change that can advance a cursor twice, and that would be my first read.

What I can't explain is the cadence. In the eight runs I pulled, the short nights are 23, 26 and 29 August: every third night, with no weekly alignment and nothing about a three-day period that falls out of a 24-hour window bug. Either that regularity is a coincidence in a small sample or it's the actual signature, and those two readings lead to completely different investigations. There are 30 rows in `job_runs` and I only looked at 8; the rest settle it either way, and that is the cheapest next step.

Two things would make this diagnosable rather than inferable. Log `window_start`, `window_end` and the length of `rows` from `fetch_events` on every run, since right now `rows_written` is the only observable and it can't distinguish a narrow window from a full window over thin data. Then, for one of the short nights, re-run `fetch_events` by hand over the full 24-hour window and compare the count to what the job recorded. If the manual count is ~48k, the window is wrong and the cursor is where to look. If it's ~23k, the events simply weren't in the source yet at 02:00, and the question becomes what upstream job is late every third night.
