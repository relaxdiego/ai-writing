I didn't find the cause, so treat what follows as the strongest available hypothesis rather than a diagnosis. The useful thing the run history gives us is that the short nights aren't random noise: of the last eight runs, three wrote roughly half the rows (23.9k, 22.1k, 23.6k against ~48k), and each of those three also took about half as long, roughly seven minutes against fifteen, and all three were recorded `ok`. That pairing is the important part. If rows were being lost during the write phase, the job would still spend the full fifteen minutes chewing through the input. Instead something upstream is handing `run()` about half as much work, and the job then completes normally on that smaller input. The short runs fall on 23, 26 and 29 August, every third day, but eight runs is far too few to call that a period, and I pulled thirty rows of history without reading past the most recent eight.

Two mechanisms fit the shape. The window in `scheduler.py` is `last_success_at or (now - timedelta(hours=24))`, so if `last_success_at` is ever persisted as something other than the previous run's `window_end`, the window shrinks and the row count follows. I'm sceptical of this one for two reasons: halving needs a twelve-hour error, which is a strange magnitude for a bookkeeping bug, and a short window should leave a gap that the following night reclaims, yet the long runs sit steadily around 48k rather than spiking to 72k. That points at the second mechanism. Commit `9d02e1e` added retries around transient fetch errors, and I never opened `fetch_events` or the retry wrapper. If that path catches a failure partway through a paginated or sharded fetch and returns the rows gathered so far instead of re-raising, the job groups a truncated result, writes it, commits cleanly, and reports success. That accounts for all four observations at once: half the rows, half the duration, no error status, and no self-healing on the following night.

What separates the two is cheap to obtain. For one of the short nights, compare the count of source events actually present in `[window_start, window_end]` against `rows_written`, and have the job log the window it computed rather than inferring it. A full 24-hour window sitting next to ~48k available source events puts the loss squarely inside `fetch_events` and its retry path, which is where I'd read next.
