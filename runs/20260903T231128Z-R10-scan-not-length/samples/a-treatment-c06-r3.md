Two things about that still need settling, and neither is the grace period argument again. The first is that "immediate" is a claim about the whole system rather than about one row, and the parts of the system that don't obey a `DELETE` are the ones that will embarrass us later. Nightly backups will still hold the account until the snapshot ages out, and you can't surgically edit a snapshot, so the choice is either to shorten backup retention to something we're comfortable stating publicly, or to keep a suppression list so a restore re-applies deletions on the way back up. The suppression list is itself a small retained record of the user's ID, which is worth knowing before we promise otherwise in the UI copy. Outside our own database, deletion has to fan out to whatever we've handed the data to: the payment processor, the mailing list, analytics, and error tracking are the usual set. Those APIs are asynchronous and some of them are slow, so our database is immediate and the fan-out finishes when it finishes.

The second is that a few records genuinely have to survive a deletion request. Invoices and transaction records are normally retained under tax law, and GDPR's erasure right carves out processing required by a legal obligation for exactly this reason, so keeping them is not the pattern you're objecting to. I'd treat that as a narrow exception covering billing records and nothing else, and say so in the confirmation dialog rather than in a footnote, since a user who reads "we delete everything" and later gets an invoice will reasonably feel lied to. Worth having someone with actual legal standing confirm the retention window before we ship the copy.

Because the action is irreversible, I'll gate it behind a typed confirmation rather than a single click, and send a deletion receipt to the address on file afterwards so an account taken over by someone else leaves a trace with the real owner.

Unless you tell me otherwise, I'll build it as: confirm, delete the account and its data synchronously, queue the processor fan-out, retain billing records only, and add a suppression list keyed on user ID so backup restores don't resurrect anyone. If you'd rather cut backup retention instead of keeping the suppression list, that's a change to infrastructure policy rather than to this button, and I'll need to know the current retention window to say what it costs.
