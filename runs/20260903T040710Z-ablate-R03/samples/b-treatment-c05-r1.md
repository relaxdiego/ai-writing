I did not find the cause, so here is what the evidence narrows it to and where I stopped.

The failure is clean and periodic rather than ragged. In the last eight runs, three came in at roughly half the row count and roughly half the wall-clock time: 23 August, 26 August and 29 August, at 23.5k, 22.1k and 23.9k rows against a normal 48k, and at about seven minutes against a normal fifteen. Every one of them was recorded as `ok`. A three-day spacing across three consecutive occurrences is suggestive but not yet a pattern I trust, because `job_runs` only holds 30 rows for this job and I did not go back far enough to see whether the cadence holds or when it started.

The proportional drop in runtime is the most informative part. If the job were failing partway through the write loop, the run would be short in rows but the fetch and grouping would still have taken full time; instead both halve together, which says the job is being handed about half as much input and is faithfully processing all of it. The write path also looks unlikely on its own terms, since `write_rollup` emits one row per account inside a single `transaction()`, so `rows_written` is counting accounts, not events, and a halving means something closer to half the accounts had no events at all in `grouped`. A partial commit would not produce that shape.

That points at `fetch_events`, and the thing I most want to look at is commit 9d02e1e, "fix: retry transient fetch errors". Retry wrappers around paginated fetches are a common source of exactly this signature: an error mid-iteration is caught, the partial result is returned as if complete, and the job reports success. I did not open `fetch_events` or read that diff, so this is a hypothesis about a plausible mechanism rather than something I confirmed. The window calculation in `scheduler.py` is the other candidate I considered and could not rule out, though it fits worse: `last_success_at or (now - timedelta(hours=24))` would shift the window rather than empty half of it, and a shifted window should push the missing accounts into the following night, which the 29th-to-30th pair does not show.

The query that would settle it is a count of source events bucketed by hour for the night of the 29th against the night of the 30th. If the events were present in the source at 02:00 on a short night, the job dropped them and the retry path is the place to look; if they arrived later, the problem is upstream of this job and the window logic becomes the real suspect.
